### Key Findings from Peer-Reviewed Sources

1. **Black rats were widespread in medieval Europe**, especially in warmer, trade-heavy cities. Ancient DNA studies show they colonized Europe twice—once during the Roman period and again in the medieval period—closely mirroring patterns of urbanization and trade [1](https://www.mpg.de/18595905/0503-evan-spread-of-black-rats-was-linked-to-human-historical-events-150495-x).

2. **Urban conditions in medieval cities**—dense housing, poor sanitation, abundant grain storage, and lack of pest control—would have created ideal environments for rat proliferation. These conditions are similar to those in modern cities with high rat-to-human ratios (e.g., Paris, London, New York), where estimates range from **0.16 to over 2.5 rats per person** [2](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0162678).

3. **Cesaretti et al. (2016)** analyzed population-area relationships for 173 medieval European cities and found strong densification patterns, implying high concentrations of food waste and human activity—both key drivers of rat populations [2](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0162678).

### Inference Basis

The **1:1 or higher rat-to-human ratio** in medieval cities is a **reasoned extrapolation**, not a documented historical fact. It is based on:
- Modern urban rat ecology.
- Historical urban density and sanitation.
- Archaeological and genomic evidence of rat presence and turnover.

The generation time of wild rats (and mice) on evolutionary relevant time scales is poorly known. If we assume, for example, a generation time of 4 generations per year (mentioned without source in Jeffares et al. 2006), this would result in a halving of μ and a doubling of Ne. These two effects exactly cancel each other with respect to the dating and relative extent of the bottleneck in the PSMC analysis. Note that Halligan et al. (2013) used the same generation time of 2/year in estimating Ne of the M. m. castaneus population, so the relative difference between both species would not be affected.

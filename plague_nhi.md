(We are asking the ‘flea’ expert about flea movement -which I can update you in a day or so, but here’s what the literature to date says)

Here’s what we know:

1. Rat fleas stay on their host until it dies, then and typically only then, they will seek other hosts (rat or human).

2. Interesting aside, rat fleas are one of the FEW fleas that like both human and rat blood equally well (so maybe sharing occurred more naturally - we don’t know). For now I would assume point 1.

3. The pla depleted strains change 2 things. 
		A - the overall mortality is reduce so total number of rats dying is much less from 0% to 20%. 
		B - a slight delay from subcutaneous infection to death - from 3-4 days to 10 days so double the time (but see point 5/6 below) which makes sense (the delay that is), if you consider that pla is required to ‘get rid' of the clot forming from the bite’ and move freely to the lymph node.

Best to think of this observed delay (to infection) as more of an “incubation” period (that is, migration to and replication in the lymph node) without being infectious to the flea (i.e. no transmission). So the rat could in theory travel further - to new locations - but rats don’t do this, they are fairly localized rodents. Bridging hosts (deer mice or other) could be important here - but we don’t want to add that variable.

4. The bacteraemic load has to be high enough in rat blood (last day typically) for the flea to pick up the bacterium and transmit it to another rat (or not). Once the rat dies (from said high load) the flea jumps (to rat or human) and the transmission cycle continues.

6. Thus Pla + and Pla depleted strains don’t behave differently at those terminal phases. That is there is NO difference between Pla + and Pla dep at the transmission stage (that we’ve been able to show).

I really think that the threshold (and thus selective pressure) is the actual rat population size. (Until they show me the pneumonic data and prove me wrong :)
